The problem

Students are juggling a maze of portals, apps and links.

A national survey of 1,010 U.S. college students found the cost shows up in missed deadlines, lost time and a weaker sense of belonging.

75%

prefer reaching student services through one central platform. Only 12% prefer separate systems.

47%

have missed a critical deadline, like a payment or registration, because they didn't know it was due.

65%

want an easier way to find groups and clubs. Only 33% use school technology to find them today.

60%

spend more than five minutes finding essentials like a class schedule or financial aid details.

Source: Pathify, 2025 Student Digital Experience Survey, reported by EDUCAUSE.

Ask ROAM

Questions answered from the school's own pages.

"Is the dining commons open?" "When are finals?" "Where can I get tutoring?" Each answer comes only from the university's published hours, calendar, catalog and offices, with the source linked under it. Crisis words always bring up 988 and 911 first.

My Day and For You

The gaps between classes, put to use.

My Day shows today's classes and where they are, then fills each gap with what fits: lunch where it's open, a study space, an event that ends before the next class. For You ranks campus events by the student's department first, then Student Affairs and the clubs they follow, with this week on one page and later weeks one swipe away.

Classes and reminders

A full schedule in under a minute.

Type "micro" and pick Principles of Microeconomics from the full catalog, or upload a calendar file and confirm the classes it finds. One tap then adds every class through the end of term to the phone's own calendar, with an alert before each one. It skips breaks and follows make-up days.

Community and resources

Every club, office and service, easy to find.

Student organizations, campus offices and departments, grouped and searchable, each with its own page. Resources answer "is it open right now?" for dining, study spaces, health, tutoring and transit, with crisis lines always on top.

For universities

A pilot your IT team doesn't have to build.

Campus ROAM starts from what you already publish: the events calendar, the student organization directory, dining and library hours and the course catalog. The first semester needs your approval and your students, not an integration project.

  • Offices and clubs post directly. Staff get their own login to publish events and announcements straight into students' feeds.
  • Your colors, your name. Each campus sets its own colors once. Text contrast adjusts automatically so it stays readable.
  • Pilot reports built in. Sign-ups, weekly active students, the screens they use most, surveys and feedback, exportable as a spreadsheet file.
  • Any phone, no app store. Opens in the browser and installs to the home screen. It keeps working when the signal drops.

Privacy and accessibility

Built to hold as little as possible, and to work for everyone.

Read the privacy policy and terms of use. A written security and accessibility summary is available on request.

  • No grades, financial aid, holds or student ID numbers. It never connects to student records.
  • Database rules let each student read and change only their own data.
  • Usage is counted as feature totals, only with consent, and never sold. One tap deletes an account and everything in it.
  • Zero violations in an automated WCAG 2.2 AA audit of every screen, in light and dark mode, plus keyboard, zoom and reduced-motion checks.

Questions

What people ask first.

What does it cost?

The pilot is free for students and for the university. Pricing for a whole campus will be set from the pilot's results.

How long does it take to set up a campus?

A few weeks. Campus ROAM starts from information the school already publishes, so there is no integration project to begin.

Does it replace our portal or events platform?

No. It brings their content into each student's day and links back to them, so more students use what the school already has.

What student data does it hold?

A school email, first name, campus, year, major, interests, the classes a student adds and the events they save. It holds no grades, financial aid or ID numbers and never connects to student records.

Is it an official Georgia Southern app?

No. It is an independent student project that uses publicly available information, and it says so inside the app.

What happens if the project stops?

Students can delete their account and everything in it at any time. If the project ends, accounts and data are deleted and participants are told by email first.
